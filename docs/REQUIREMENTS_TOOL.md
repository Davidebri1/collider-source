> "when we get to this, i want us to develop a gui tool with universal
> applicability. upload project folder/zip, requirements file is detected or
> selected, requirements are parsed out and grouped by area and nested as
> necessary (high level requirements become dependencies / requirements in all
> related child requirements. all generated by deriving intent and resolving to
> logical conclusion. no pattern match necessary. simple binary problem
> solving. guaranteed transfer of value."
>
> "but this will be for later once we get the app to a useful place."

## What it does

1. **Input:** a project folder or zip.
2. **Detect or select** the requirements file within it.
3. **Parse requirements out** of that file.
4. **Group by area** — the surface each requirement serves, not a stage number.
5. **Nest by dependency.** A high-level requirement becomes a dependency of,
   and therefore a requirement within, every child requirement related to it.
   A parent that is unmet marks every child unmet.
6. **Derive intent, then resolve to the logical conclusion.** Not pattern
   matching against phrasing. The meaning of a requirement is derived, and
   only checked against the code once derived.
7. **Binary outcome per requirement.** Met or not met. No score, no
   percentage, no confidence weighting.

## The design principle behind it

Requirements get lost when they are abstracted away from the thing they
describe. A requirement filed under "Stage 5" cannot be found from the screen
it governs, so the screen gets worked on many times without it ever surfacing.
This tool exists so that a requirement is always reachable *from its area*, and
so coverage is a derived fact rather than someone's recollection.

"Guaranteed transfer of value" is the acceptance bar: a requirement that goes
in must come out the other side attached to the thing it constrains, with no
step in between where it can be summarised away.

## Why binary and not scored

Same reason `scripts/qualify-smartgen.mjs` produces transcripts and no
aggregate: a score lets a partially-met requirement read as progress. Met or
not met forces the question "what is missing" instead of "how close are we."
